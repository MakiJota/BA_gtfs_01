Big regional feeds (e.g. Västtrafik for Gothenburg) have hundreds of bus lines. Untick “Bus” and keep the busiest lines to stay musical.

2 · Play

--:--

Space = play/pause · scroll to zoom · drag to pan · R = reset view. Play a moment loops the time you pick, so you can compare 7 am with 3 pm.

Save & share

Stores the city, BPM, speed, scale, time and every line's note, instrument, sound on/off and number of trains, so whoever opens it hears exactly what you hear. You can also drop a .maki file on the page.

3 · Lines → notes

All lines start muted: turn on the ones you want with 🔇, or Unmute all. Note = the first station; going along the line climbs the scale. − n + = trains running now. Click a line (here or on the map) to launch one more train.

About me

Hi! My name is Jonathan.

I work with digitization and data analytics, using data to explore and communicate insights that address urban challenges. I enjoy diving into new data sets and finding creative ways to make sense of complex information.

This site is a little side quest that combines two things I love: cities and music. It turns public-transport timetables into sound. Every train that reaches a station plays a note.

The idea stands on the shoulders of Conductor by Alexander Chen and AMBA Trenes Sonoros by Juan Torchia. See the full credits.

If you have comments, suggestions, or want to collaborate on a similar project, feel free to reach out. I'm always happy to talk data, cities, and tech!
